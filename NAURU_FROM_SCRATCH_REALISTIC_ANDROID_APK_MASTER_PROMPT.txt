3. WORLD SCALE: SOLVE THE AIRCRAFT CONFLICT EXPLICITLY
------------------------------------------------------

Use a hybrid 1:25 game-world interpretation:

- Global island locations and travel distances use 1:25 compression.
- One real-world metre of geographic separation becomes 0.04 game metres.
- Local gameplay units still represent metres, kilograms, and seconds.
- The human, vehicle dimensions, road widths, doors, selected building footprints, vegetation, street furniture, and collision clearances remain believable human scale.
- Cull minor structures that overlap after compression. Never stack thousands of full-size buildings at compressed positions.

A literal 1:25 runway is too short for believable aeroplane physics. Therefore implement an “airport hero zone” rather than cheating the flight model:

- Preserve the real runway center, compass alignment, and relationship to the coast.
- Locally expand the distance along the runway axis so the playable runway is approximately 600–900 game metres long, suitable for a light STOL propeller aircraft.
- Use a smooth spatial falloff around the airport so the local warp does not create a visible discontinuity.
- Apply the same world transform to terrain, runway, nearby roads, collision, minimap data, and object placement.
- Cull or reposition minor overlapping airport-side structures after the warp.
- Keep road widths, runway width, markings, lights, and buildings human scale.
- Document this deliberate game-world distortion in README.md.

One possible transform is:

    compressed = realLocalPosition * 0.04
    along      = dot(realLocalPosition - runwayCenter, runwayAxis)
    lateral    = abs(dot(realLocalPosition - runwayCenter, runwayRight))
    influence  = smoothstep(airportOuterRadius, airportInnerRadius, lateral)
    extra      = runwayAxis * along * (airportAlongScale - 0.04) * influence
    gamePos    = compressed + extra

Choose airportAlongScale from the measured real runway length so the transformed runway falls in the 600–900 m target range. Verify continuity and ensure that all systems call a single WorldTransform implementation.

Provide at least 2–4 km of playable ocean/airspace around the compact island so boats, helicopters, and aeroplanes have room to operate. Use a floating origin if the playable flight radius grows enough to affect float precision.

6. SIMULATION FOUNDATION
------------------------

All local physics uses SI units:

- Distance: metres.
- Mass: kilograms.
- Time: seconds.
- Speed: metres/second internally, km/h or knots in the HUD.
- Force: newtons.
- Torque: newton-metres.

Use a fixed physics timestep independent from rendering:

    fixedDt = 1 / 60 second

Recommended accumulator behavior:

    accumulator += min(realFrameDt, 0.1)
    substeps = 0
    while accumulator >= fixedDt and substeps < 4:
        apply controls and forces
        step Bullet by fixedDt
        run post-physics synchronization
        accumulator -= fixedDt
        substeps += 1
    render interpolation alpha = accumulator / fixedDt

Do not multiply forces by variable frame time and then feed them to a fixed-step engine. Keep control sampling, force application, physics stepping, and render interpolation separate.

Physics architecture:

- Collision layers for terrain, static architecture, human, car, boat, helicopter, aeroplane, water trigger volumes, interaction triggers, and debris.
- Static triangle meshes only for terrain/architecture.
- Convex/compound colliders for dynamic vehicles.
- Continuous collision detection for fast aircraft when needed.
- Sleeping for inactive rigid bodies.
- High-fidelity simulation only for the occupied vehicle and nearby active objects.
- Lower-rate proxy simulation or sleep for distant unoccupied vehicles.
- Deterministic pure force-model classes separated from Bullet adapters so they can be unit tested without native physics.

Implement a hybrid character system, not a sliding camera capsule.

Core controller:

- Human-scale capsule collider, approximately 1.7–1.9 m tall.

- Different traction for asphalt, dirt, wet ground, shallow water, and steep rock.

      jumpVelocity = sqrt(2 * gravity * desiredJumpHeight)

- Fall damage/hard-landing response based on impact velocity, with safe thresholds.
- The body must push lightweight dynamic objects through bounded impulses without launching vehicles.
- Enter/exit each vehicle through seat and exit transforms, with collision disabled only during the transition.

Animation:

- Rigged original humanoid with idle, walk, run, sprint, jump, fall, land, swim, enter/exit, driving, boating, helicopter, and aeroplane poses.
- Blend animation speed to physical speed.
- Foot IK to terrain on high/balanced settings.
- Head/torso aim limits and camera-relative turning.
- Hands align to steering wheel, boat wheel/tiller, helicopter controls, and aeroplane controls where practical.

Ragdoll:

- Use a reduced rigid-body ragdoll only for severe impacts or explicit testing.
- Blend from animation to ragdoll without teleporting limbs.
- Put ragdolls to sleep and despawn/reset safely.

Human acceptance scenarios:

- Walk up normal road slopes without jitter.

- Step over a curb but not through a wall.

8. CAR PHYSICS
--------------

Create an original compact 4x4 appropriate for island roads. Use a rigid chassis with four raycast wheels or a carefully validated Bullet vehicle implementation.

Required model:

- Realistic mass, center of mass, dimensions, inertia, and wheelbase.
- Per-wheel suspension spring and damper:

      springForce = springRate * compression
      damperForce = damperRate * relativeSuspensionVelocity
      normalLoad  = clamp(springForce - damperForce, 0, maxLoad)

- Wheel angular velocity and inertia.
- Engine torque curve versus RPM.
- Idle, redline, throttle response, engine braking, clutch approximation, forward gears, neutral, and reverse.
- Final drive and differential torque distribution.
- Speed-sensitive steering and approximately Ackermann inner/outer wheel angles.
- Longitudinal slip ratio and lateral slip angle.
- Tire force curves that saturate rather than increasing without limit.
- Friction circle/ellipse combining braking/acceleration and cornering force.
- Surface-dependent grip for asphalt, dirt, grass, wet road, sand, and shallow water.
- Brake balance, handbrake on rear wheels, traction control/ABS assists as optional settings.
- Aerodynamic drag and rolling resistance.
- Anti-roll effect or physically stable suspension geometry.
- Collision damage state that affects appearance and handling without requiring soft-body deformation.

Visual synchronization:

- Wheel rotation from angular velocity.
- Front-wheel steering.
- Suspension travel visible on each wheel.
- Chassis pitch/roll comes from physics, not a canned animation.
- Brake lights, headlights, dust/spray particles, and tire audio tied to physical state.

Car acceptance scenarios:

- Settles under gravity without bouncing forever.
- Accelerates plausibly through gears.
- Stops from 50 km/h within a documented plausible range on dry asphalt.
- Understeers/oversteers progressively rather than snapping randomly.
- Loses grip on grass/wet sand.
- Cannot tunnel through buildings at maximum speed.
- Remains stable at 30 FPS rendering because physics stays fixed at 60 Hz.


9. BOAT PHYSICS
---------------

Create an original small island workboat or center-console boat.

Required model:

- One rigid hull with 6–12 distributed buoyancy sample points.
- At each point, compare hull point height with the analytic rendered wave height.
- Apply buoyancy proportional to displaced volume and submersion:

      buoyancy = waterDensity * displacedVolume * gravity

- Apply force at each sample point so waves create pitch and roll naturally.
- Add vertical damping to prevent perpetual bobbing.
- Hydrodynamic drag separated into forward, lateral, and vertical components.
- Strong lateral resistance below the waterline, but allow controlled slip.
- Propeller thrust based on throttle, engine RPM, propeller submersion, and forward speed.
- Rudder/steering force increases with water flow and becomes weak at zero speed unless the design has vectored thrust.
- Planing lift approximation at higher speed, tuned to avoid numerical explosions.
- Wind drag above the waterline.
- Shallow-water and beaching detection.
- Hull collision with piers, reefs, coast, and other vehicles.
- Engine cuts or thrust reduces when the propeller is out of water.
- Wake, spray, foam, pitch/roll audio, and engine audio driven by physical state.

Boat acceptance scenarios:

- Floats at the designed waterline with a stable but not frozen response.
- Recovers from moderate roll and pitch.
- Turns more effectively while moving than while stationary.
- Slows naturally after throttle release.
- Rides the same wave function that is rendered.
- Beaches without falling through terrain or climbing cliffs.
- Does not gain energy indefinitely from damping or buoyancy errors.


10. HELICOPTER PHYSICS
----------------------

Create an original light utility helicopter. Use a rigid fuselage and an aerodynamic rotor force model; do not fake flight by directly setting position.

Required state/model:

- Fuselage mass, inertia tensor, center of mass, main rotor position, tail rotor position, skids, and drag areas.
- Rotor RPM spool-up/spool-down and a minimum flight RPM.
- Collective controls total main-rotor thrust.
- Cyclic tilts the rotor thrust vector and creates pitch/roll moments.
- Main rotor reaction torque acts on the fuselage.
- Tail rotor or anti-torque system counters torque and provides yaw control.
- Translational lift increases efficiency as horizontal airflow develops.
- Ground effect increases rotor efficiency near a broad surface and fades smoothly with height.
- Parasite drag increases with airspeed.
- Vertical descent through low-forward-speed rotor wash should reduce lift in a bounded vortex-ring approximation.
- Autorotation may be simplified but rotor RPM must not instantly remain powered after throttle/engine loss.
- Skid contacts and suspension/compliance prevent explosive landing jitter.
- Optional stability augmentation should apply bounded torques, not teleport orientation.
- Wind affects the fuselage and rotor inflow.

A useful starting approximation is:

      thrustMagnitude = Ct * airDensity * rotorArea * tipSpeed^2 * collective
      thrustDirection = normalized(rotorUp + cyclicPitchAxis + cyclicRollAxis)
      mainRotorForce  = thrustDirection * thrustMagnitude

Tune Ct and control authority from documented target mass and hover collective. Keep coefficients in a named HelicopterConfig asset, not scattered magic numbers.

Helicopter acceptance scenarios:

- Requires rotor spool before takeoff.
- Can hover near a documented collective value without automatic position locking.
- Cyclic produces acceleration by tilting thrust, not by setting velocity.
- Pedal/yaw control counters main-rotor torque.
- Ground effect is noticeable but not a launch impulse.
- Hard landings bounce/damage appropriately; gentle skid landings settle.
- Engine power loss causes descent and rotor behavior rather than suspended flight.
- Cannot pass through terrain or the airport buildings at normal speed.


11. AEROPLANE PHYSICS
---------------------

Create an original light single-engine propeller STOL aeroplane suitable for the expanded airport hero zone. Do not use a jet or an aircraft requiring a runway longer than the game provides.

Required model:

- Rigid fuselage with mass, center of mass, inertia, wing area/span/chord, tail geometry, propeller, and landing gear points.
- Compute local airflow at aerodynamic surfaces from linear velocity, angular velocity, wind, and prop wash where relevant.
- Dynamic pressure:

      q = 0.5 * airDensity * airspeed^2

- Lift:

      lift = q * wingArea * Cl(angleOfAttack, flapSetting)

- Drag:

      drag = q * wingArea * (Cd0 + inducedDragFactor * Cl^2)

- Cl must rise through the normal range, peak near the stall angle, and decline after stall. Never allow unlimited lift at extreme angle of attack.
- Separate wing, horizontal stabilizer, and vertical stabilizer contributions when practical.
- Elevator, aileron, rudder, trim, and flap effects.
- Propeller thrust versus engine RPM, throttle, and airspeed.
- Engine spool/response and optional mixture simplified for mobile controls.
- Ground effect near the runway.
- Three landing-gear contact/suspension points with rolling friction, braking, and nose/tail steering as appropriate.
- Wheel spin and gear compression visuals.
- Crosswind and weather influence.
- Structural/damage thresholds for overspeed, excessive G, and hard landing, kept simple enough for mobile.

Aeroplane acceptance scenarios:

- Accelerates down the transformed runway under wheel physics.
- Takes off within the documented target distance without a hidden upward impulse.
- Requires sufficient airspeed and angle of attack for lift.
- Stalls when slow/high-alpha, drops a wing or loses control authority in a bounded recoverable way, and recovers when airflow is restored.
- Aileron controls roll, elevator controls pitch, rudder controls yaw.
- Flaps reduce takeoff/landing speed while increasing drag.
- Can land, compress its gear, brake, and remain on the runway.
- Does not fly when engine thrust is present but airspeed/lift conditions are absent.
- Uses continuous collision detection or swept checks at high speed.

14. THERMAL, BATTERY, AND PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------

Excessive phone heat is a release blocker.

Frame pacing:

- Do not use an unrestricted continuous 60/90/120 FPS loop by default.
- Target approximately 30 FPS in the normal balanced mobile profile.
- Drop to 20 FPS when Android battery saver is active or thermal status reaches moderate.
- Drop to approximately 15 FPS at severe thermal status.
- Pause rendering and physics correctly when the Activity is paused or the screen turns off.
- Respect Android screen timeout; do not force an unattended game to keep the display/GPU awake.
- Poll thermal state at a low frequency or use a listener; do not call a system service every frame.

ECO response:

- Reduce dynamic resolution.
- Disable or simplify sampled dynamic shadows.
- Select the tightest sector and LOD ranges.
- Reduce particles, local lights, reflection updates, vegetation distance, and animation update rate.
- Keep controls and occupied-vehicle physics responsive; do not lower fixed physics accuracy so far that vehicles become unstable.

Suggested balanced-profile budgets on a modest 1080p-class phone:

- Rendering: 30 FPS / 33.3 ms frame budget.
- Main simulation and game logic: ideally below 6 ms average.
- Visible triangles: roughly 150k–350k after LOD, depending on GPU.
- Draw calls: preferably below 150–200.
- Dynamic rigid bodies awake near player: below approximately 100.
- Shadow-casting local lights: zero; one main directional shadow system.
- Runtime texture residency: target below approximately 256 MB on balanced.
- Java/native memory must be measured; avoid per-frame allocations and garbage-collection spikes.

Optimization requirements:

- Sector/quadtree streaming and frustum culling.
- Mesh LOD and collision LOD.
- Texture atlases, mipmaps, compressed texture formats when supported, and a safe fallback.
- Instancing for repeated vegetation/props if verified on target GLES3 hardware.
- Object pools for particles and transient objects.
- No per-frame creation of arrays, matrices, strings, vectors, or physics shapes in hot paths.
- Sleep distant bodies and update distant animation at reduced frequency.
- Profile CPU and GPU before guessing.
- Add an in-game diagnostics overlay showing FPS, frame time, draw calls, visible sectors, visible triangles, active bodies, and current thermal profile in development builds.

Graphics profiles:

LOW:
- 20–30 FPS cap, low dynamic resolution, 512 px/no shadow depending on capability, short vegetation/building range, low particles.

BALANCED:
- Default 30 FPS, compact shadow map with throttled updates, moderate LOD ranges, normal materials.

HIGH:
- Still capped unless the user deliberately unlocks it; improved shadows/LOD/reflections but thermal protection always overrides.

ECO:
- Automatic battery/thermal override shown in the HUD; user cannot bypass severe thermal protection.


15. SAVE, RECOVERY, AND FAILURE HANDLING
----------------------------------------

- Version the save format and world transform.
- Save character position, current vehicle, all vehicle spawn/recovery state, time, selected graphics/assist settings, and last safe checkpoint.
- Reject old/incompatible coordinates instead of spawning outside the transformed island.
- Keep periodic saves off the render thread.
- If world construction fails, show an error screen with retry rather than closing.
- If a noncritical texture/model fails, substitute a visible fallback and log the asset path.
- If the occupied vehicle falls out of bounds, provide a deterministic recover-to-last-safe-location action.
- Never catch and silently ignore fatal world corruption.


16. TESTING REQUIREMENTS
------------------------

A. Pure unit tests

Geodata:
- Binary magic/version/count bounds.
- Projection and 1:25 transform.
- Smooth airport hero-zone transform.
- Coastline bounds and point-in-polygon.
- Representative-building spacing and determinism.
- Mesh/index limits.

Physics force models:
- Human jump velocity and slope classification.
- Suspension spring/damper signs and force limits.
- Tire slip-force saturation and friction ellipse.
- Boat buoyancy equilibrium and damping energy loss.
- Helicopter hover force near weight, reaction torque, and control sign.
- Aeroplane lift/drag curves, stall peak/decline, flap effect, and control-surface sign.
- Fixed-step result remains close across different render frame sequences.

B. Packaged-resource startup test

This test must load the resources from the built Android variant, construct the complete CPU world, and verify:

- Real map and elevation files exist.
- World transform version and scale.
- Airport hero-zone runway length and alignment.
- Character and all four vehicles have valid, non-overlapping spawn locations.
- Character/car spawn is walkable.
- Boat spawn is in navigable water.
- Helicopter spawn has rotor clearance.
- Aeroplane spawn sits on the runway and aligns with it.
- Required representative landmarks and districts exist.
- Selected mesh builders remain within supported index limits.
- No NaN/infinite positions, normals, masses, inertia, or configuration values.

C. Physics integration scenarios

Run deterministic headless or instrumentation scenarios where supported:

- Human walk/jump/land.
- Car suspension settle, acceleration, turning, braking, collision.
- Boat static float, throttle, turn, wave response.
- Helicopter spool, hover, cyclic movement, yaw, landing.
- Aeroplane runway acceleration, takeoff, controlled stall, recovery, landing roll.

Log numeric outcomes and fail outside documented tolerances. Do not rely only on screenshots.

D. Android build/runtime QA

- Clean Gradle build on GitHub Actions.
- Run all unit and packaged-resource tests before staging the APK.
- Install and launch on an emulator when the CI environment supports it.
- Capture logcat and detect FATAL EXCEPTION, ANR, native tombstones, shader compile failures, EGL errors, and missing resources.
- Treat emulator infrastructure failure separately from app failure.
- A CI emulator is not a substitute for testing heat and controls on a physical phone.
- If Android tooling is unavailable locally, do not fabricate results; use CI and persist diagnostics in the repository.

E. APK validation

Before delivery:

- Verify ZIP integrity.
- Verify AndroidManifest package, launcher activity, version, min SDK, and target SDK.
- Verify all DEX files and native libraries for intended ABIs.
- Verify map, elevation, models, textures, shaders, and license/attribution resources are packaged.
- Verify APK signing (debug v2/v3 is acceptable for direct testing).
- Compute SHA-256 and save a sidecar .sha256 file.
- Only publish the named APK after required compile and startup tests pass.


17. GITHUB ACTIONS BUILD WORKFLOW
---------------------------------

Create a workflow that runs on the working branch and manual dispatch:

1. Checkout source.
2. Set up a maintained Java 17 action.
3. Set up the verified Gradle version or use a committed Gradle wrapper.
4. Reset old QA status files so a stale PASSED result can never approve a new build.
5. Run a clean compile/assemble task.
6. Run unit tests and the packaged-world startup test, capturing logs.
7. Optionally run emulator launch QA with diagnostics.
8. Require the current startup status to equal PASSED before staging the APK.
9. Copy the APK to a clear versioned filename, for example:

      Nauru-The-Countryside-REALISTIC-PHYSICS-v1.0.0.apk

10. Generate the SHA-256 sidecar.
11. Upload the APK as a GitHub Actions artifact.
12. If repository policy allows binary artifacts, publish the tested APK and diagnostics to the working branch with a skip-CI commit.
13. If tests fail, persist diagnostics but never publish the failed APK as the final deliverable.

Do not embed credentials. Use the repository-provided GitHub token and minimum required permissions.


18. MANDATORY INCREMENTAL MILESTONES
------------------------------------

Do not attempt every system in one giant untested change. Each milestone ends with a compiled installable APK and tests.

MILESTONE 0 — Toolchain proof
- Clean project, launcher, GLES3 clear screen, one lit mesh, Bullet initialization, touch input, CI APK.
- Exit criterion: APK compiles and launcher/startup test passes.

MILESTONE 1 — Real transformed Nauru + human
- Geodata compiler, terrain, coast, water, roads, selected structures, minimap, attribution, human controller, camera, saves.
- Exit criterion: player walks a recognizable transformed Nauru; packaged-world startup test passes.

MILESTONE 2 — Car
- Full car force model, model/LOD/collider, controls, camera, enter/exit, audio basics, tests.
- Exit criterion: acceleration/turn/brake/suspension scenarios pass and APK remains stable.

MILESTONE 3 — Boat
- Harbor/boat spawn, analytic waves, buoyancy/hydrodynamics, controls, beaching, enter/exit, tests.
- Exit criterion: float/turn/wave scenarios pass.

MILESTONE 4 — Helicopter
- Airport/helipad spawn, rotor physics, controls/assist, landing, camera, tests.
- Exit criterion: spool/hover/yaw/landing scenarios pass.

MILESTONE 5 — Aeroplane + airport hero zone
- Smooth airport transform, expanded runway, light STOL aircraft, flight model, gear, takeoff/stall/landing tests.
- Exit criterion: physically generated takeoff and landing are repeatable within the runway.

MILESTONE 6 — Visual realism and interaction polish
- Final original vehicle art, human animations, PBR-lite materials, landmarks, audio, particles, weather/wind integration, UI.
- Exit criterion: no placeholder critical vehicle meshes in the release candidate.

MILESTONE 7 — Thermal/performance release pass
- Profiling, culling/LOD, dynamic resolution, ECO behavior, memory audit, shader fallback, final QA.
- Exit criterion: 30/20/15 FPS pacing works as designed, no unrestricted loop, no startup regression, final APK validated.

At every milestone, keep the previous known-good APK until the new one passes.

NOTES FOR THE PERSON USING THIS PROMPT
--------------------------------------

1. This prompt deliberately specifies a light STOL aeroplane. A realistic airliner cannot use a globally compressed 1:25 island/runway without either unrealistic flight physics or a much larger airport zone.
2. Realistic multi-vehicle physics and polished original art are a large project. The milestone gates prevent an agent from producing thousands of untested lines and only discovering at the end that the APK does not open.
3. A successful emulator build does not prove that a phone will remain cool. Always test the final APK on the intended physical phone while monitoring frame rate, battery drain, Android thermal status, and control stability.
4. If maximum visual quality is more important than APK size, allow larger original glTF models and textures, but preserve LODs, compression, thermal ECO behavior, and memory budgets.
5. Keep all map and asset licenses with the project so the APK can be redistributed legally.
